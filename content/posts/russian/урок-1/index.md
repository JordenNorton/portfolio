## Building basic Russian questions using ‘что’, ‘это’, and ‘кто’

{{< note >}}Note: This blog is a space for thinking in Russian, not just translating. You’ll see some English to help with context, but try not to rely on it too much. Let your brain get comfy with Russian, connect words to ideas, and resist the urge to translate word-for-word.{{< /note >}}

{{< keyvocab >}}
<span class="ru">Что</span> — <span class="en">What</span>  
<span class="ru">Это</span> — <span class="en">This / It</span>  
<span class="ru">Кто</span> — <span class="en">Who</span>
{{< /keyvocab >}}

<p>
Diving into some of the most common and useful Russian questions today - things like:

    “What is this?”, “Who is that?”, “This is a ticket!”

These are core questions that pop up all the time in real conversations, and they’re surprisingly simple once you get the hang of the structure.
</p>


Starting with the basics. The question Что это? literally translates to “What is this?” but in Russian, there’s no need for a verb like “is”. It’s just implied.

{{< callout type="tip" title="Grammar Tip" >}}
<p>Asking “Что это?” is just like saying “What is this?” in English. But in Russian, word order is simpler and articles like “a” or “the” don’t exist.</p> <p>For example:</p> <p><span class="vocab-pair"><span class="ru">Это билет</span> <span class="en">(This is a/the ticket)</span></span></p> <p><span class="vocab-pair"><span class="ru">Это офис</span> <span class="en">(This is the office)</span></span></p> {{< /callout >}}

"Это" is used a lot in everyday speech. It can mean “This is,” “It is,” or even just “This”—all depending on context.

Let’s see it in action.

{{< dialogue >}}
<p><span class="speaker-a">Анна:</span> Что это?</p>
<p><span class="speaker-b">Борис:</span> Это кофе.</p>
<br>
<p><span class="speaker-a">Анна:</span> Извините, что это?</p>
<p><span class="speaker-b">Борис:</span> Я не знаю, что это.</p> {{< /dialogue >}}

Try reading this without translating. Just imagine you’re Anna, holding something unfamiliar, trying to figure it out.
